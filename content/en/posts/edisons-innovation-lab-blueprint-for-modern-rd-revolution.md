## Context

These sources explore the life and legacy of **Thomas Edison**, highlighting his transition from an independent inventor to the pioneer of the **modern industrial research laboratory**. The 
texts document his most famous achievements, such as the development of the **phonograph** and the **incandescent light bulb**, while emphasizing the critical role of **applied chemistry** in 
his success. Beyond Edison, the collection provides a historical analysis of **Research and Development (R&D)** in the United States, tracing its evolution from nineteenth-century machine shops 
to the massive **military-industrial-university complex** of the Cold War. The narrative examines how funding shifted over time from government-led initiatives for **national security** toward 
private-sector investments focused on **commercialization and healthcare**. Ultimately, the sources illustrate how structured innovation has shaped the American economy and transformed the way 
technology is integrated into daily life.
