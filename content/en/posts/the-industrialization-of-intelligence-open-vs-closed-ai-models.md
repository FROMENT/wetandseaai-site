## Executive Summary

The performance gap between open-weight AI models and proprietary frontier systems has compressed to 3–4 months, a significant acceleration from previous trajectories. This convergence is driven by three concurrent dynamics: improved model distillation techniques, hardware efficiency gains, and maturation of open-source training pipelines. Inference costs for open models have declined by an order of magnitude annually, reducing deployment overhead and lowering entry barriers for enterprises. The strategic value proposition has shifted from model architecture gatekeeping toward data integration, fine-tuning infrastructure, and regulatory compliance frameworks. Organizations must reassess infrastructure spending, vendor lock-in risk, and data sovereignty considerations as the AI market industrializes beyond research-phase access control.

## Key Points

- **Performance convergence stabilizes at 3–4 months lag** — Open-weight models trained on commodity hardware now match proprietary capabilities within a predictable timeframe, contrasting with the 12–18 month gaps observed in 2022–2023.

- **Inference cost reduction: 10x annual decline** — Lower computational overhead enables marginal deployment costs for CPU-bound and GPU-optimized inference, eliminating cost as a primary barrier to adoption for mid-market deployments.

- **Model distillation and efficiency engineering** — Techniques including knowledge distillation, quantization, and sparse architectures explain performance-cost trade-offs without requiring proprietary training data or scaling laws previously reserved for closed ecosystems.

- **Limitation: domain-specific performance gaps persist** — Open models may still underperform in specialized reasoning tasks, multimodal alignment, and long-context retrieval; proprietary fine-tuning remains advantageous for edge cases requiring frontier capability.

- **Strategic shift toward data moats and integration** — Competitive advantage now accrues to organizations controlling data pipelines, domain-specific corpora, and rapid fine-tuning workflows rather than base model access; implications for cybersecurity include reduced reliance on vendor-supplied security updates and increased responsibility for supply-chain validation of open components.
