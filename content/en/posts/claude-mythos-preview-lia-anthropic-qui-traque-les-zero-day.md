## Executive Summary

Anthropic has unveiled **Project Glasswing**, a controlled defensive security initiative centered on Claude Mythos Preview, its most capable model to date. The system demonstrates autonomous discovery of critical zero-day vulnerabilities across major operating systems and web browsers—capabilities Anthropic determined too sensitive for public release. Instead, access is restricted to a consortium of twelve strategic partners including AWS, Google, and Microsoft, supported by $100 million in security credits and funding for open-source infrastructure hardening. This approach reflects a deliberate governance choice: leveraging frontier AI for proactive threat mitigation while constraining availability to mitigate offensive weaponization risks. The initiative raises substantive questions about vulnerability disclosure timelines, responsible AI deployment in high-stakes security domains, and the role of private technology consortia in critical infrastructure protection.

## Key Points

- **Claude Mythos Preview autonomously identifies zero-day vulnerabilities** at scale across operating systems and major browsers, exceeding manual security research capacity and enabling defenders to patch before exploit chains develop.

- **Restricted access via consortium model**: Anthropic granted controlled deployment only to twelve pre-vetted partners (AWS, Google, Microsoft, and others), rejecting public availability due to offensive misuse potential and maintaining direct oversight of vulnerability disclosure workflows.

- **$100 million in direct support** allocated to security infrastructure, open-source projects, and mission-critical codebases, operationalizing the defensive gains by strengthening ecosystem resilience rather than consolidating discovery within proprietary systems.

- **Regulatory and geopolitical friction**: The initiative has triggered debate regarding dual-use AI capabilities, AI safety precedent-setting, and whether defensive AI deployment creates liability or disclosure acceleration obligations for participating organizations.

- **Operational impact on incident response timelines**: Organizations outside the consortium face delayed access to patches, creating a privileged security tier that may exacerbate vulnerability windows for non-partner infrastructure and raise fairness concerns in critical systems protection.
