## Contexte

This **NBER working paper** examines the economic consequences of **artificial intelligence**, focusing specifically on its 
potential to worsen **income inequality** and **unemployment**. The authors, **Korinek and Stiglitz**, argue that while AI can
significantly expand production, it often acts as a **labor-saving technology** that threatens to replace human workers and 
depress wages. They provide a **taxonomy of welfare outcomes**, illustrating that **technological progress** only benefits 
everyone if the government implements robust **redistributive policies** and institutional reforms. Without such 
interventions, the **surplus gained by innovators** may come at the direct expense of the working class. Ultimately, the paper
suggests that **non-distortionary taxation** and changes to **intellectual property rights** are necessary tools to prevent AI
from creating a permanent underclass or a **Malthusian catastrophe**.
